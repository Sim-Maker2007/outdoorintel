Garmin vs OnX : quel système GPS pour les chasseurs canadiens?

Getting lost in the Canadian wilderness isn't romantic – it's dangerous. Here's the GPS system that keeps you safe and on track.

Table of Contents - Why You Need GPS for Hunting - Standalone GPS vs. Phone Apps - Garmin GPS Devices - OnX Hunt App - Feature Comparison - Battery Life Reality - Frequently Asked Questions - Final Recommendations

Why You Need GPS for Hunting

Paper maps fail when: - You're in whiteout conditions - It's pitch black at 5 PM - You're tracking a blood trail at dusk - Weather destroys your map

Your phone fails when: - No cell signal (most hunting areas) - Battery dies in cold - Screen breaks - Water damage

A dedicated GPS or offline mapping app is essential safety equipment, not a luxury.

Standalone GPS vs. Phone Apps

Standalone GPS (Garmin)

Pros: - Works without cell signal - Purpose-built for outdoors - Rugged and waterproof - Replaceable batteries - Years of battery life (device, not usage)

Cons: - Expensive - Separate device to carry - Smaller screens than phones - Map updates cost money

Phone Apps (OnX)

Pros: - Large, bright screens - Always with you - Regular free updates - More intuitive interface - Multiple map layers

Cons: - Battery drain - Fragile (breakable) - Requires downloading offline maps - Cold weather kills batteries

Garmin GPS Devices

Best Overall: Garmin GPSMAP 66sr

Garmin GPSMAP 66sr GPS

Price: $450-500 CAD Screen: 3" color, sunlight-readable Battery: 36 hours (GPS mode) Features: Multi-band GNSS, BirdsEye imagery, inReach compatible

Why It Wins: - Most accurate GPS reception (multi-band) - Preloaded TOPO maps - Satellite imagery capability - Rugged construction - inReach integration for emergencies

Best For: Serious hunters, backcountry expeditions

Best Budget: Garmin eTrex 22x

Garmin eTrex 22x GPS

Price: $220-260 CAD Screen: 2.2" color Battery: 25 hours Features: Preloaded TOPO maps, basic but reliable

Why It's Good: - Affordable entry into Garmin ecosystem - Uses AA batteries (easy to replace) - Rugged and waterproof - Sufficient for most hunting

Best For: Beginners, occasional hunters, backup unit

Best for Emergency: Garmin inReach Mini 2

Garmin inReach Mini 2

Price: $450-500 CAD + subscription ($20-65/month) Weight: 100g Features: SOS, two-way texting, GPS tracking, weather

Why It's Essential: - Sends SOS to emergency services - Text family from anywhere - GPS tracking lets family follow your location - Works globally via Iridium satellite

Subscription Options: - Safety: $20/month (unlimited SOS) - Recreation: $40/month (unlimited texting) - Expedition: $65/month (unlimited everything)

Best For: Solo hunters, remote areas, safety-conscious

OnX Hunt Premium

OnX Hunt Premium Subscription

Battery Life Reality

Garmin GPSMAP 66sr

  • GPS Mode: 36 hours
  • Expedition Mode: 450 hours
  • AA Batteries: Easy to carry spares
  • Cold Weather: Minimal impact

Phone with OnX

  • Active Use: 4-6 hours
  • Airplane Mode + GPS Only: 8-12 hours
  • Power Bank: Essential (carry 10,000+ mAh)
  • Cold Weather: 50% reduction in battery life

Battery Solutions for Phone Users

Power Bank: Anker PowerCore 10000 - Price: $30-40 CAD - Capacity: 2-3 full phone charges - Essential for OnX users

Phone Warmers: Hand warmers taped to phone case - Prevents cold shutdown - Cheap insurance

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do I need both Garmin and OnX?

A: Many hunters use both: - OnX: Pre-trip planning, private land info, scouting - Garmin: Backup navigation, emergency use, battery reliability

Q: Can I use OnX without cell signal?

A: Yes, BUT you must download offline maps BEFORE leaving cell coverage. Download your hunting area while on WiFi.

Q: Is OnX worth the subscription?

A: For $40/year, absolutely. The private land boundary data alone is worth it. Know exactly where you can legally hunt.

Q: Does Garmin require subscription?

A: Basic GPS functions: No. BirdsEye satellite imagery: Yes ($30/year). inReach messaging: Yes ($20-65/month).

Q: What about free alternatives?

A: CalTopo: Free, excellent for planning Gaia GPS: Free tier available Google Maps: Can download offline areas

These work but lack hunting-specific features (land ownership, hunting zones).

Q: Can I use my phone's built-in GPS?

A: Yes, but: - Not as accurate as dedicated GPS - Battery drains fast - No offline maps without apps - Fragile

Fine for casual use, not recommended for serious backcountry hunting.

Q: Do I need satellite communicator?

A: If you hunt alone in remote areas, YES. If you always hunt with partners with cell coverage, less critical. But for $20/month, why risk it?

Final Recommendations

Best Overall System: Garmin GPSMAP 66sr + OnX Hunt

Garmin for reliable navigation and emergency backup. OnX for planning, scouting, and land boundaries. Together they cover all bases.

Budget Option: OnX Hunt + Power Bank

If you can't afford Garmin, OnX with a good power bank is viable. Download maps, carry backup battery, protect phone from weather.

Safety Essential: Garmin inReach Mini 2

Even if you use OnX for navigation, consider an inReach for emergencies. One SOS call justifies the cost.

The Truth About Hunting GPS

Getting lost is expensive (search and rescue costs thousands). Getting found quickly can save your life. Whether you choose Garmin, OnX, or both, invest in reliable navigation. Your family will thank you.
