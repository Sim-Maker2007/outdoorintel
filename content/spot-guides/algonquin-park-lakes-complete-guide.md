# Algonquin Park Lakes: The Complete Canoe-in Fishing Guide

*2,400 lakes, 1,200 km of canoe routes, world-class brook trout. Here's everything you need to know about fishing Ontario's iconic provincial park.*

**Quick Facts**
- **Location**: Central Ontario, 3 hours north of Toronto
- **Size**: 7,653 km² (largest provincial park in Ontario)
- **Lakes**: 2,400+ (backcountry + highway accessible)
- **Rivers**: 1,200 km of canoe routes
- **Species**: Brook trout, lake trout, smallmouth bass, pike
- **Best Season**: Spring (trout), Summer (bass), Fall (all species)
- **Access**: Highway 60 corridor + backcountry canoe routes

---

## Why Algonquin is Special

Algonquin isn't just a provincial park—it's Canada's outdoor cathedral:

- **2,400 lakes**: Most never see a fishing rod
- **Brook trout stronghold**: Native populations in remote lakes
- **Backcountry wilderness**: True wilderness, car-accessible
- **120-year fishing tradition**: Conservation and tradition
- **Dark skies**: No light pollution
- **Moose, wolves, bears**: Wildlife viewing with fishing

The park offers two distinct fishing experiences:
1. **Highway 60 Corridor**: Easy access, drive-in lakes
2. **Backcountry**: Canoe-in only, virgin fishing

---

## Highway 60 Corridor (Car-Accessible)

### Best Corridor Lakes

**1. Lake of Two Rivers**
- **Access**: Drive-in, parking lot
- **Species**: Bass, pike
- **Best**: Family fishing, shore fishing
- **Pros**: Easy access, good for beginners
- **Cons**: Pressured fish

**2. Pog Lake**
- **Access**: Drive-in campground
- **Species**: Bass, pike
- **Best**: Camping + fishing combo
- **Note**: Busy in summer

**3. Whitefish Lake**
- **Access**: Highway 60 pull-off
- **Species**: Bass, pike
- **Best**: Quick shore fishing
- **Cons**: Limited shore access

**4. Rock Lake**
- **Access**: Rock Lake Campground
- **Species**: Lake trout, bass
- **Best**: Canoe rental on-site
- **Note**: Deep lake, lake trout in summer

**5. Canoe Lake**
- **Access**: Canoe Lake Access Point
- **Species**: Bass, pike
- **Best**: Gateway to backcountry
- **Note**: Historic Tom Thomson connection

## Backcountry Lakes (Canoe-In)

### Easy Access Backcountry

**1. Joe Lake**
- **Paddle**: 1 hour from Canoe Lake
- **Portages**: None (direct route)
- **Species**: Bass, pike
- **Best**: First backcountry trip
- **Campsites**: Multiple options

**2. Little Joe Lake**
- **Paddle**: 1.5 hours
- **Portages**: One short carry
- **Species**: Bass, pike, surprise brook trout
- **Best**: Less crowded than Joe

**3. Tom Thomson Lake**
- **Paddle**: 2 hours from Canoe
- **Portages**: Two
- **Species**: Bass, pike
- **Best**: Named for famous painter
- **Scenery**: Island campsites

### Medium Access Backcountry

**4. Burnt Island Lake**
- **Paddle**: 3+ hours, multiple routes
- **Portages**: 3-4
- **Species**: Bass, pike, lake trout
- **Best**: Fishing + scenery
- **Size**: Large lake, room to explore

**5. Big Trout Lake**
- **Paddle**: 6+ hours or multi-day
- **Portages**: 6+
- **Species**: Lake trout, brook trout
- **Best**: Trophy potential
- **Remote**: True wilderness

### Trophy Brook Trout Lakes

**6. Opeongo Lake**
- **Access**: Boat launch (motorboats allowed)
- **Species**: Lake trout, brook trout, bass
- **Best**: Largest lake in park, multi-species
- **Size**: 58 km², deep, cold

**7. Cedar Lake**
- **Access**: North end of park
- **Paddle**: Long access
- **Species**: Brook trout, lake trout
- **Best**: Native brook trout

**8. Various Unnamed Lakes**
- **Access**: Bushwhack or faint portages
- **Species**: Brook trout (often virgin populations)
- **Best**: True wilderness experience
- **Challenge**: Hard to find, harder to reach

## Species Breakdown

### Brook Trout (The Prize)

**Where**: Remote, cold lakes
**When**: Spring (ice-out) and fall
**How**: Fly fishing, spinning, live bait (where legal)
**Size**: 12-18 inches typical, 20+ inches trophy

**Best Lakes**:
- High elevation lakes
- Spring-fed lakes
- Lakes with no other fish species
- Unnamed remote lakes

**Tactics**:
- **Fly fishing**: Dry flies at dawn/dusk
- **Spinning**: Small spoons, spinners
- **Live bait**: Worms, minnows (check regulations)

**The Experience**: Native brook trout in Algonquin are the real deal. Brightly colored, aggressive, wild.

### Lake Trout

**Where**: Deep lakes (Opeongo, Burnt Island)
**When**: Spring (shallow), summer (deep), fall (shallow)
**How**: Trolling, jigging
**Size**: 2-6 lbs typical, 10+ lbs possible

**Tactics**:
- **Trolling**: Downriggers or leadcore
- **Jigging**: Heavy jigs in deep water
- **Spoon trolling**: Metal spoons, slow speed

**Best Lakes**:
- Opeongo (largest population)
- Rock Lake
- Big Trout Lake

---

### Smallmouth Bass

**Where**: Warm water lakes, most corridor lakes
**When**: June-August (season opens 3rd Sat in June)
**How**: Topwater, drop-shot, crankbaits
**Size**: 1-3 lbs typical, 4+ lbs possible

**Accessibility**: Most common fish in park
**Tactics**: Standard bass techniques work

---

### Northern Pike

**Where**: Weedy bays, warm water
**When**: All season
**How**: Spoons, spinnerbaits, jerkbaits
**Size**: 2-5 lbs, 10+ lbs possible

**Aggressive**: Pike hit anything that moves

## Planning Your Trip

### Trip Types

**Day Trip**
- **Access**: Corridor lakes only
- **License**: Day pass + fishing license
- **Time**: 6-8 hours on water
- **Best**: Weekdays (less crowded)

**Weekend Camping**
- **Access**: Drive-in or easy canoe
- **Permit**: Campsite reservation
- **Lakes**: Joe, Little Joe, Rock Lake
- **Gear**: Car camping + canoe

**Multi-Day Backcountry**
- **Access**: Canoe routes only
- **Permit**: Backcountry permit
- **Rental**: Canoe at access point
- **Portage gear**: Lighter the better

### Essential Gear

**Fishing**:
- Light spinning rod (trout/bass)
- Fly rod (if targeting trout)
- Assorted lures
- Net (rubber-coated)
- Pliers
- Measuring tape

**Camping**:
- Portage-friendly gear
- Bear-proof food storage (mandatory)
- Water filter (no tap water in backcountry)
- Satellite communicator (inReach recommended)

**Safety**:
 - First aid kit
- Map + compass
- Whistle
- Rain gear
- Layers (mountain weather)

## Regulations Specific to Algonquin

### Fishing License
- Standard Ontario license required
- Outdoors Card + license
- Conservation or Sport license options

**Buy**: huntandfishontario.com or visitor centre

### Park Permits
- **Day**: $21/day (2026), includes fishing parking
- **Camping**: $43/night (interior), $54 (comfort stations)
- **Seasonal**: $210/year

**Buy**: Ontario Parks reservation system

### Special Regulations
- **Brook trout**: Check zone-specific rules
- **Lake trout**: Sport license recommended
- **Live bait**: Restricted in some lakes
- **Park zones**: Algonquin divided into zones

**Full regulations**: ontarioparks.com/parks/algonquin

## Wildlife

### Moose
- **Where**: Shorelines, wetlands
- **When**: Dawn/dusk
- **Safety**: 30m minimum distance
- **Canoe encounters**: Occasional swimmer

### Bears
- **Black bears**: Common
- **Precaution**: Bear-proof food storage
- **Hanging**: Required in backcountry

### Wolves
- **Heard often**: Howling at dusk
- **Seen rarely**: Timid of humans

### Loons
- **Iconic**: Algonquin sound
- **Common**: Most lakes
- **Nesting**: May-July (give space)

## Best Times to Visit

### Spring (May-June)
**Pros**:
- Bug-free (mostly)
- No crowds
- Trout fishing excellent
- Waterfalls flowing

**Cons**:
- Some roads closed
- Cool weather
- Campsites not all open

### Summer (July-August)
**Pros**:
- All facilities open
- Warm weather
- Swimming
- Family time

**Cons**:
- Crowded (book early)
- Bugs (July especially)
- Hot days

### Fall (September-October)
**Pros**:
- Fall colours
- No bugs
- Less crowded
- Moose rutting season
- Great fishing

**Cons**:
- Cold nights
- Shorter days
- Some services closed

### Winter
**Pros**:
- Skiing/snowshoeing
- Wolves active
- Frozen waterfalls
- No bugs

**Cons**:
- Limited access
- Cold
- Not fishing season (mostly)

## Safety Considerations

### Backcountry Risks
- **Getting lost**: Occurs annually
- **Weather changes**: Mountain weather
- **Cold water**: Hypothermia risk
- **No cell service**: Most of park
- **Bears**: Food storage critical

### Safety Essentials
- [ ] Trip plan (file with park)
- [ ] First aid training
- [ ] Emergency equipment
- [ ] Communication device
- [ ] Navigation (not just phone)
- [ ] Weather awareness

### Emergency
- **Park staff**: Contact visitor centres
- **911**: Works in some corridor areas
- **Satellite communicator**: Recommended for backcountry

## Final Thoughts

Algonquin represents the best of Canadian wilderness—accessible yet wild, developed yet pristine. The fishing ranges from easy family fun on corridor lakes to true expedition fishing in the backcountry.

For brook trout purists, Algonquin is holy ground. Native speckled trout in their natural habitat, accessible by canoe, in landscapes that inspired the Group of Seven.

Plan well. Respect the wilderness. Practice Leave No Trace. And experience one of Canada's greatest natural treasures.

**Book early for summer. Learn portaging for backcountry. And catch a wild brook trout at sunrise.**

*See you in the park.*
