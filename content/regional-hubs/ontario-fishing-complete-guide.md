# Ontario Fishing: The Complete Guide to Canada's Best Angling Province

*240+ lakes, rivers, and streams. World-class walleye, bass, trout, and muskie. Here's everything you need to know about fishing in Ontario.*

**Quick Facts**
- **Provincial Capital**: Toronto
- **Population**: 14.5 million
- **Water Area**: 250,000+ lakes (1/6 of world's fresh water)
- **License Cost**: $26.57 (residents), $51.46 (non-residents)
- **Best Seasons**: Year-round (ice fishing in winter)
- **Trophy Potential**: World records caught here annually

---

## Why Ontario is Special

Ontario isn't just Canada's most populous province—it's an angler's paradise:

- **1/6 of world's fresh water** (Great Lakes + 250,000+ inland lakes)
- **Four of five Great Lakes** border Ontario (Superior, Huron, Erie, Ontario)
- **World-class walleye fishing** (Santee Cooper of the North)
- **Trophy smallmouth bass** (6+ lb fish common)
- **Brook trout stronghold** (native populations in remote areas)
- **Muskie capital** (more 50+ inch fish than anywhere)
- **Ice fishing mecca** (4-5 month hardwater season)

Whether you want to catch a 10-lb walleye on Lake Nipissing, battle a 6-lb smallmouth on the Ottawa River, or trek to a remote brook trout lake in Algonquin Park—Ontario has it all.

## Best Fishing Regions

### 1. Southern Ontario (Golden Horseshoe)
**Location**: Toronto to Niagara to Windsor
**Best For**: Bass, pike, carp
**Access**: Excellent (population centers)

**Top Spots**:
- Lake Simcoe (ice fishing, lake trout, whitefish)
- Lake Ontario tributaries (salmon, steelhead runs)
- Grand River (trout, carp, bass)
- Bay of Quinte (trophy walleye)

**Why Fish Here**: Close to Toronto. World-class fishing within 1-2 hours of 6+ million people.

---

### 2. Eastern Ontario (Ottawa Valley)
**Location**: Ottawa to Pembroke
**Best For**: Bass, walleye, muskie
**Access**: Very Good

**Top Spots**:
- Ottawa River (trophy smallmouth, walleye, muskie)
- Rideau River (chain of lakes, multiple species)
- Mississippi River (bass, walleye)
- Madawaska River (trout, remote feel)

**Why Fish Here**: Ottawa River is bass fishing paradise. Free public access. Montreal and Ottawa nearby.

---

### 3. Central Ontario (Cottage Country)
**Location**: Muskoka, Haliburton, Kawarthas
**Best For**: Bass, lake trout, walleye, muskie
**Access**: Good (cottage roads)

**Top Spots**:
- Muskoka Lakes (Big 3: Muskoka, Rosseau, Joseph)
- Haliburton Highlands (100+ lakes)
- Kawartha Lakes (chain system, excellent bass)
- Lake of Bays (lake trout, bass)

**Why Fish Here**: Classic Canadian shield lakes. Clear water, rocky structure, trophy potential.

---

### 4. Northern Ontario (Algoma & North Bay)
**Location**: Sudbury, North Bay, Sault Ste. Marie
**Best For**: Walleye, lake trout, northern pike, brook trout
**Access**: Moderate (longer drives)

**Top Spots**:
- Lake Nipissing (Ontario's 5th Great Lake, walleye)
- French River (muskie, walleye, historic waterway)
- Lake Huron North Shore (lake trout, salmon)
- St. Joseph Island (bass, pike)

**Why Fish Here**: Less pressured. Bigger fish. Remote wilderness feel.

---

### 5. Northwestern Ontario (Sunset Country)
**Location**: Kenora, Dryden, Fort Frances
**Best For**: Walleye, lake trout, muskie
**Access**: Challenging (very remote)

**Top Spots**:
- Lake of the Woods (1 million acres, world-class everything)
- Eagle Lake (trophy muskie, walleye)
- Wabigoon Lake (walleye factory)
- Rainy Lake (U.S./Canada border, excellent multi-species)

**Why Fish Here**: Canada's best multi-species fishing. Trophy potential in every species. Remote lodges.

---

### 6. Northeastern Ontario (Cochrane & Timmins)
**Location**: Cochrane, Timmins, Kirkland Lake
**Best For**: Brook trout, walleye, northern pike
**Access**: Difficult (fly-in or very long drives)

**Top Spots**:
- Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater (trophy brook trout)
- Mattagami River (walleye, remote)
- Various remote lakes (brook trout, lake trout)

**Why Fish Here**: True wilderness. Native brook trout. No crowds.

## Species-by-Species Guide

### Walleye (Pickerel)

**Ontario Status**: Provincial fish. World-class populations.

**Best Lakes**:
1. Bay of Quinte (trophy factory)
2. Lake Nipissing (Ontario's walleye capital)
3. Lake of the Woods (abundant + big fish)
4. Ottawa River (river walleye fish like lake fish)
5. Wabigoon Lake (numbers game)

**Best Seasons**:
- **Spring**: Spawn fishing (late April-May, zone dependent)
- **Summer**: Deep water structure
- **Fall**: Aggressive pre-winter feed
- **Winter**: Ice fishing bonanza

**Tactics**: Jigging, trolling, live bait rigging

### Smallmouth Bass

**Ontario Status**: Explosive growth since 1990s. Now world-class.

### Largemouth Bass

**Ontario Status**: Abundant in southern Ontario. 7+ lb fish possible.

**Best Lakes**:
- Lake Scugog (trophy factory)
- Rice Lake (shallow, weedy)
- Sturgeon Lake (Kawarthas)
- Various southern Ontario ponds/lakes

**Tactics**: Frogs, flipping, crankbaits, spinnerbaits

### Lake Trout

**Ontario Status**: Native populations in deep lakes. Trophy potential.

**Best Lakes**:
1. Lake Simcoe (ice fishing mecca)
2. Lake of Bays (Muskoka)
3. Lake Temagami (deep water lakers)
4. Lake Superior (trophy potential, challenging)
5. Various deep shield lakes

**Tactics**: Downrigging, jigging, trolling spoons

### Brook Trout (Speckled Trout)

**Ontario Status**: Native. Best populations in remote areas.

**Best Areas**:
1. Algonquin Park (accessible brook trout)
2. Algoma Country (Sault Ste. Marie area)
3. Temagami region (remote lakes)
4. Northeastern Ontario (fly-in territory)

4. St. Lawrence River (100+ mile fishery)

## License & Regulations

### Fishing License (2026)

| Type | Resident | Non-Resident |
|------|----------|--------------|
| 1-Year Sport | $26.57 | $51.46 |
| 1-Year Conservation | $14.71 | $29.32 |
| 7-Day Sport | $15.08 | $31.23 |
| 7-Day Conservation | $8.55 | $17.71 |
| 1-Day Sport | $11.14 | $22.02 |

**Outdoors Card**: $8.57 (one-time, 3-year card)

**Where to Buy**:
- Online: huntandfishontario.com
- ServiceOntario locations
- Most bait shops
- Canadian Tire

### Conservation vs Sport License
- **Sport**: Full limits (higher possession limits)
- **Conservation**: Reduced limits (lower impact fishing)

### Key Regulations

**Bass Season**: Opens 3rd Saturday in June (varies by zone)
**Walleye Season**: Open all year (most zones)
**Muskie Season**: 1st Saturday in June to December 15
**Lake Trout**: Open all year (most zones)
**Brook Trout**: Zone dependent

**Check zones**: Ontario uses FMZ (Fisheries Management Zones). Regulations vary by zone.

**Full regulations**: ontario.ca/fishing

## Best Seasons to Visit

### Spring (April-June)
**Pros**:
- Walleye spawn fishing
- Steelhead/salmon runs (tributaries)
- Less boat traffic
- Pre-spawn bass aggressive

**Cons**:
- Unpredictable weather
- Some lakes still ice-covered (north)
- Black flies (late May-June)

**Best For**: Walleye, trout, pre-spawn bass

---

### Summer (July-August)
**Pros**:
- Warm weather, comfortable fishing
- Family vacation season
- All species active
- Stable conditions

**Cons**:
- Crowded (popular lakes)
- Hot weather (midday lull)
- Heavy boat traffic

**Best For**: Bass, lake trout (deep), family trips

---

### Fall (September-October)
**Pros**:
- Aggressive feeding (winter preparation)
- Muskie peak season
- Beautiful fall colors
- Less boat traffic
- Stable weather

**Cons**:
- Shorter days
- Cold mornings
- Some lodges closing

**Best For**: Muskie, walleye, fall bass

---

### Winter (November-March)
**Pros**:
- Ice fishing world-class
- No bugs
- Unique experience
- Accessible lakes (walk on)

**Cons**:
- Cold weather
- Gear intensive
- Safety concerns
- Limited species

**Best For**: Lake trout, walleye, whitefish, perch

## Planning Your Trip

### Trip Types

#### Day Trip from Toronto
- **Lake Simcoe**: 1 hour, ice fishing or boat
- **Kawartha Lakes**: 1.5 hours, bass fishing
- **Grand River**: 1 hour, trout/salmon

#### Weekend Getaway
- **Muskoka**: 2 hours, luxury cottages + fishing

## Conservation & Ethics

### Ontario's Fish populations
Most Ontario fisheries are healthy due to:
- Strict regulations
- Slot limits (protect breeding stock)
- Seasonal closures
- Habitat protection

### Responsible Angling
- **Know your limits**: Check zone regulations
- **Handle fish properly**: Wet hands, quick release
- **Don't transplant bait**: Invasive species risk
- **Clean your gear**: Prevent spread of invasives
- **Respect other anglers**: Don't crowd

### Invasive Species Concerns
- **Zebra mussels**: Clean boats/trailers
- **Round goby**: Don't use as bait
- **Asian carp**: Report sightings
- **Spiny water flea**: Clean lines
