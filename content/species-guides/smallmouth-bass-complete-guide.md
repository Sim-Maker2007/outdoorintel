# The Complete Guide to Smallmouth Bass Fishing in Canada

*The bronze bomber. The smallie. Pound-for-pound the hardest fighting fish in fresh water. Here's everything you need to know about catching smallmouth bass.*

**Quick Facts**
- **Scientific Name**: Micropterus dolomieu
- **Other Names**: Bronzeback, smallie, brown bass
- **Native Range**: Great Lakes, St. Lawrence River
- **Introduced**: Throughout Canada
- **Trophy Size**: 18+ inches, 4+ lbs
- **Canadian Record**: 9.84 lbs (Beaverton Harbour, 1954)

---

## Why Smallmouth Bass Are Special

Smallmouth bass have earned a cult following among Canadian anglers:

- **Explosive fighters**: Jump, run, never give up
- **Accessible**: Found in 90% of southern Ontario waters
- **Trophy potential**: 5-6 lb fish in many lakes
- **Year-round fishing**: Open season June-October (Ontario)
- **Visual feeders**: Aggressive surface strikes
- **Smart fish**: Learn patterns, challenge anglers

Unlike largemouth bass that hide in weeds, smallmouth relate to structure—rocks, points, drop-offs. They're the perfect target for anglers who like to move, explore, and fish actively.

## Biology & Behavior

### Physical Characteristics
- **Color**: Bronze-gold sides, dark vertical barring
- **Mouth**: Upper jaw doesn't extend past eye (vs. largemouth)
- **Eyes**: Red eye (distinctive)
- **Size**: Average 1-2 lbs, trophy 4-6 lbs
- **Lifespan**: 10-12 years

### Habitat Preferences
- **Depth**: 2-25 feet (varies by season)
- **Structure**: Rock, gravel, points, drop-offs
- **Current**: Often found near current breaks
- **Water clarity**: Prefers clear to stained water
- **Temperature**: 65-80°F (18-27°C) optimal

### Behavior Patterns

**Feeding**
- **Visual predators**: Sight-feed primarily
- **Diet**: Crayfish, minnows, insects, frogs
- **Aggressive**: Attack lures larger than their mouth
- **Smart**: Learn quickly, pressured fish get wary

**Spawning**
- **When**: Water 60-65°F (15-18°C), late May-June
- **Where**: Gravel nests, 2-8 feet deep
- **Duration**: Male guards nest 2-3 weeks
- **Catch & release**: Critical during spawn

**Seasonal Movements**
- **Pre-spawn**: Shallow, aggressive feeding
- **Spawn**: On beds, protective
- **Post-spawn**: Deep water, recovering
- **Summer**: Deep structure (10-20 ft)
- **Fall**: Shallow again, aggressive feed

## Best Smallmouth Bass Waters in Canada

### Ontario

**1. Ottawa River**
- **Why**: 6+ lb fish, river smallies fight harder
- **Where**: Ottawa to Pembroke stretch
- **How**: Drop-shotting, crankbaits
- **Trophy potential**: Highest in Ontario

**2. Lake Simcoe**
- **Why**: Deep smallies, clear water
- **Where**: Rocky points, deep structure
- **How**: Jigging, drop-shotting
- **Size**: 4-5 lb fish common

**3. St. Lawrence River**
- **Why**: 100+ miles of bass water
- **Where**: From Kingston to Quebec border
- **How**: Casting, trolling
- **Bonus**: Multi-species (pike, muskie)

**4. Kawartha Lakes**
- **Why**: Chain of lakes, different options
- **Where**: Buckhorn, Pigeon, Stoney
- **How**: Variety of techniques
- **Access**: Easy from Toronto

**5. Lake of the Woods**
- **Why**: Northwestern Ontario gem
- **Where**: Rock shorelines
- **How**: Casting tubes, jerkbaits
- **Size**: 4-6 lb fish

### Other Provinces

**Lake Champlain (Quebec)**
- World-class smallmouth
- Tournament destination
- Border lake (U.S./Canada)

**Various Manitoba Lakes**
- Introduced populations
- Growing rapidly
- Eastern Manitoba lakes

## Seasonal Smallmouth Tactics

### Spring (Pre-Spawn)

**When**: Water 50-60°F (10-15°C)
**Where**: Secondary points, staging areas
**How**: Slow presentation, pre-spawn feeding

**The Bite**: Smallmouth feed aggressively before spawn. Best time of year for numbers AND size.

**Tactics**:
- **Jerkbaits**: Suspending baits, pause retrieves
- **Crankbaits**: Shallow divers (4-8 ft)
- **Jigs**: 3/8 oz with craw trailer
- **Swimbaits**: Slow roll near bottom

**Key Locations**:
- Points near spawning bays
- Creek channels
- Transition areas

### Spring (Spawn)

**When**: Water 60-65°F (15-18°C)
**Where**: Gravel beds, 2-8 feet
**How**: Sight fishing, bed fishing

**The Bite**: Protective males attack anything near nest.

**Tactics**:
- **Soft plastics**: Tubes, creature baits
- **Drop-shot**: Precise placement
- **Ned rig**: Subtle presentation
- **Sight fishing**: Polarized glasses essential

**Ethics**: Catch and release during spawn. Don't target same fish repeatedly.

**Key Locations**:
- Gravel flats
- Near spawning bays
- Visible beds

### Summer

**When**: Water 70-85°F (21-29°C)
**Where**: Deep structure (10-25 ft)
**How**: Drop-shotting, jigging, deep cranking

**The Bite**: Challenging. Smallmouth retreat to deeper, cooler water.

**Tactics**:
- **Drop-shot**: Roboworms on deep points
- **Jigging**: Football jigs on structure
- **Deep cranking**: 10-20 ft divers
- **Tube jigging**: Dragging bottom

**Time of Day**: Early morning, late evening (low light)

**Key Locations**:
- Main lake points
- Deep humps
- Break lines
- Rock piles

### Fall

**When**: Water 55-65°F (13-18°C)
**Where**: Shallow to mid-depth
**How**: Aggressive presentation

**The Bite**: Smallmouth feed heavily before winter. Aggressive and accessible.

**Tactics**:
- **Topwater**: Whopper Plopper at dawn/dusk
- **Crankbaits**: Shallow to medium divers
- **Spinnerbaits**: Wind-blown banks
- **Jigs**: Heavy cover

**Key Locations**:
- Shallow feeding flats
- Rock piles
- Wind-blown banks
- Creek mouths

## Smallmouth Techniques

### Drop-Shotting
**When**: Clear water, deep fish
**How**: Weight below hook, bait suspended
**Bait**: Roboworms, minnows
**Setup**: 10-15 lb fluoro, #1 hook

### Jerkbait Fishing
**When**: Cold water, suspended fish
**How**: Jerk-jerk-pause retrieve
**Bait**: Vision 110, Husky Jerk
**Key**: Long pauses (3-10 seconds)

### Tube Jigging
**When**: All season
**How**: Hop and drag bottom
**Bait**: 3.5-4" tubes
**Weight**: 1/8-3/8 oz

### Crankbait Fishing
**When**: Covering water
**How**: Cast and retrieve
**Key**: Bounce off rocks/structure

### Ned Rigging
**When**: Pressured fish
**How**: Drag slowly bottom
**Bait**: TRD, small plastics
**Weight**: 1/16-1/8 oz

### Topwater Fishing
**When**: Low light, calm conditions
**How**: Walk, pop, or plop
**Best**: Dawn/dusk, overcast

## Common Mistakes

1. **Fishing too fast**: Smallmouth often want slow presentation
2. **Wrong depth**: Not using electronics to find fish
3. **Too heavy line**: Visible line in clear water
4. **Wrong lure size**: Match forage size
5. **Ignoring wind**: Wind concentrates fish
6. **Fishing same spot**: Smallmouth move, you should too

---

## Conservation

### Catch & Release Best Practices
- Use rubber nets (protect slime coat)
- Wet hands before handling
- Minimize air exposure
- Revive fish before release
- Don't lip smallmouth (different than largemouth)

### Slot Limits (Ontario)
- Check zone-specific regulations
- Many lakes: Protect 13-16 inch fish
- Goal: Preserve breeding stock

---

## Pro Tips

1. **"Follow the wind"**: Wind pushes bait, smallmouth follow

2. **"Sharp hooks"**: Smallmouth have bony mouths. Sharp hooks = better hooksets

3. **"Downsize when tough"**: Clear water/pressure = smaller lures

4. **"Current is key"**: River smallies stack on current breaks

5. **"Rock = Smallmouth"**: If there's rock, there are smallmouth

6. **"Don't overlook small water"**: Ponds and creeks hold trophy fish

7. **"Seasonal movements"**: Follow fish as they move shallow to deep

## Final Thoughts

Smallmouth bass fishing is pure joy. The strike, the fight, the acrobatics—nothing else compares in freshwater.

They're accessible enough for beginners but challenging enough for experts. Every smallmouth lake is different. Every season brings new patterns to learn.

Whether you're dropping shotting deep structure on Lake Simcoe or throwing topwaters at dawn on the Ottawa River, smallmouth bass deliver.
