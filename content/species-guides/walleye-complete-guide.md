# The Complete Guide to Walleye Fishing in Canada

*The prized "pickerel" of Canadian lakes. Here's everything you need to know about finding, catching, and cooking walleye.*

**Quick Facts**
- **Other Names**: Pickerel, yellow pike, dore (French)
- **Scientific Name**: Sander vitreus
- **Provincial Fish**: Manitoba, Saskatchewan
- **Best Season**: Spring and fall
- **Trophy Size**: 28+ inches, 10+ lbs
- **Ontario Record**: 22.25 lbs (Grassy Narrows Lake, 1943)

---

## Why Walleye Matter

Walleye are Canada's most popular game fish for good reason:

- **Delicious eating**: White, flaky, mild-tasting flesh
- **Challenging to catch**: Light biters, finicky feeders
- **Trophy potential**: 10+ lb fish in many lakes
- **Year-round fishing**: Open water and ice seasons
- **Province-wide distribution**: Found across Canada
- **Economic impact**: Drives tourism in many regions

For many Canadian anglers, walleye is the first fish they learn to target and the species they return to throughout their lives.

---

## Biology & Behavior

### Physical Characteristics
- **Eyes**: Large, reflective (the "walleye" name)
- **Color**: Olive-gold back, white belly
- **Teeth**: Sharp canine teeth
- **Size**: Average 1-3 lbs, trophy 8-15 lbs
- **Lifespan**: 10-15 years in wild

### Habitat Preferences
- **Depth**: 10-30 feet (typical)
- **Structure**: Drop-offs, points, humps
- **Bottom**: Sand, gravel, rock
- **Water clarity**: Stained to clear (not muddy)

### Behavior Patterns

**Feeding**
- Primarily feed in low-light conditions
- Ambush predators (wait for prey)
- Diet: Minnows, crayfish, insects
- "Light-sensitive" eyes help them see in dim conditions

**Spawning**
- **When**: Spring (water 42-50°F / 6-10°C)
- **Where**: Shallow gravel or rocky areas
- **Time**: Usually April-May (varies by region)
- **Duration**: 1-2 weeks

**Seasonal Movements**
- **Spring**: Shallow for spawn
- **Summer**: Deep water structure
- **Fall**: Shallow feeding (aggressive)
- **Winter**: Mid-depth, suspend

## Best Walleye Lakes in Canada

### Ontario

**1. Bay of Quinte**
- **Why**: Trophy factory, 10+ lb fish annually
- **Best**: Trolling crankbaits in fall
- **Access**: Belleville area, multiple launches

**2. Lake Nipissing**
- **Why**: Ontario's "walleye factory"
- **Best**: Ice fishing, spring spawn
- **Access**: North Bay, Callander, South Shore

**3. Lake of the Woods**
- **Why**: Numbers + size, 30+ inch fish
- **Best**: Jigging reefs and points
- **Access**: Kenora, Sioux Narrows

**4. Ottawa River**
- **Why**: River fish grow big, less pressured
- **Best**: Jigging current breaks
- **Access**: Ottawa to Pembroke

**5. Wabigoon Lake**
- **Why**: Northwestern Ontario gem
- **Best**: Numbers fishing
- **Access**: Dryden area

### Other Provinces

**Lake Winnipeg (Manitoba)**
- World-class walleye (greenback strain)
- Trophy 30+ inch fish
- Ice fishing capital

**Last Mountain Lake (Saskatchewan)**
- Saskatchewan's best walleye
- Consistent year-round
- Near Regina

**Lake Athabasca (Alberta/Saskatchewan)**
- Remote, trophy potential
- Lodge-based fishing
- 20+ lb fish possible

**Lake Erie (Ontario)**
- Great Lakes walleye
- Massive population
- Port Dover, Port Stanley

## Seasonal Walleye Tactics

### Spring (Spawn Season)

**When**: Water 42-50°F (6-10°C)
**Where**: Shallow (3-15 ft), rocky/gravel areas
**How**: Jigging, casting crankbaits

**The Bite**: Pre-spawn walleye feed aggressively. Post-spawn can be tough.

**Tactics**:
- **Jigging**: 1/4 to 3/8 oz jigs with minnows or plastics
- **Crankbaits**: Shallow divers along shorelines
- **Live bait**: Minnows on slip bobbers

**Key Locations**:
- Spawning reefs
- River inlets
- Rocky shorelines

### Summer (Deep Water)

**When**: Water 65-80°F (18-27°C)
**Where**: 15-30 ft, structure
**How**: Trolling, jigging, live bait rigs

**The Bite**: Walleye retreat to deeper, cooler water. Still feed but less aggressively.

**Tactics**:
- **Trolling**: Deep-diving crankbaits, bottom bouncers with spinners
- **Jigging**: Vertical jigging on structure
- **Live bait**: Lindy rigs or bottom bouncers with nightcrawlers

**Key Locations**:
- Main lake points
- Humps and reefs
- Deep weed edges
- Thermocline zone

**Time of Day**: Early morning and evening (low light)

### Fall (Feeding Frenzy)

**When**: Water 50-60°F (10-15°C)
**Where**: Shallow to mid-depth
**How**: Trolling, casting, jigging

**The Bite**: Walleye feed aggressively before winter. Best time for numbers AND size.

**Tactics**:
- **Trolling**: Crankbaits, spinner rigs
- **Casting**: Jigs, crankbaits to shoreline
- **Jigging**: Aggressive presentation

**Key Locations**:
- Shallow feeding flats
- Transition zones
- Current areas

**Color Patterns**: Fall colors (orange, red, gold)

### Winter (Ice Fishing)

**When**: Safe ice (December-March)
**Where**: 20-40 ft, structure
**How**: Jigging, dead sticks

**The Bite**: Slower than open water but consistent. Quality over quantity.

**Tactics**:
- **Jigging**: Aggressive jigging spoons (Jigging Rap, Hyper-Glide)
- **Dead sticks**: Live minnow on second rod
- **Tip-ups**: Large shiners for trophy hunting

**Key Locations**:
- Mid-lake humps
- Break lines
- Deep structure

**Electronics**: Flasher or underwater camera essential

## Live Bait Tips

### Minnows
- **Size**: 2-4 inches typical
- **Types**: Fatheads, shiners, chubs
- **Hooking**: Through lips or behind dorsal
- **Storage**: Keep cool and aerated

### Nightcrawlers
- **Presentation**: Whole or half
- **Rigging**: Spinner rigs, Lindy rigs
- **Advantage**: Stay on hook, scent trail

### Leeches
- **Best**: Summer months
- **Size**: Medium (2-3 inches)
- **Rigging**: Jig or Lindy rig

## Cooking Walleye

### Cleaning
1. Remove scales (if keeping skin)
2. Fillet both sides
3. Remove rib cage
4. Skin or leave on (personal preference)

### Best Cooking Methods

**Pan Fried**
- Dredge in flour or cornmeal
- Butter or oil, medium heat
- 2-3 minutes per side

**Deep Fried**
- Beer batter or cornmeal
- 350°F oil
- Until golden brown

**Baked**
- Lemon, butter, herbs
- 400°F for 15-20 minutes

**Grilled**
- Foil packet with vegetables
- Medium heat, 10-15 minutes

### Recipes
- Classic shore lunch
- Walleye tacos
- Lemon butter walleye
- Walleye chowder

## Conservation

### Slot Limits
Many Ontario lakes use "slot limits":
- **Protected slot**: Must release fish between certain sizes (e.g., 16-22 inches)
- **Goal**: Protect breeding-age fish
- **Check regulations**: Varies by lake

### Catch & Release Tips
- Use rubber nets (protects slime coat)
- Wet hands before handling
- Quick release (don't keep out of water)
- Barbless hooks (optional, but helpful)

### Sustainable Harvest
- Keep eating-size fish (under 18 inches)
- Release trophies (over 25 inches)
- Don't take more than you'll eat

## Common Mistakes

1. **Fishing too fast**: Walleye often prefer slow presentations
2. **Wrong depth**: Not using electronics to find fish
3. **Too much hardware**: Simple rigs often work best
4. **Wrong time**: Fishing midday in summer (too bright)
5. **Poor hooksets**: Walleye have soft mouths (don't horse them)

---

## Pro Tips

1. **"The night bite"**: Some of the best walleye fishing is 1 hour before/after sunset

2. **"Follow the wind"**: Wind pushes bait, walleye follow

3. **"Color matters"**: Gold in stained water, silver in clear

4. **"Sharp hooks"**: Walleye have bony mouths. Sharp hooks = better hooksets

5. **"Stay mobile"**: If no bites in 15-20 minutes, move

6. **"Electronics"**: Good fish finder catches 3x more walleye

7. **"Moon phases"**: Full moon = good night fishing

## Final Thoughts

Walleye fishing is a lifelong pursuit. Some days you'll limit out in an hour. Other days you'll grind for a single fish. That's walleye fishing.

The key is persistence, adaptability, and respect for the fish. Learn their patterns. Invest in good electronics. Keep experimenting with techniques.

And most importantly—enjoy the experience. Whether you're fishing a remote lake in Northwestern Ontario or your local reservoir, walleye fishing connects you to Canada's natural heritage.

**Tight lines and sharp hooks.**
