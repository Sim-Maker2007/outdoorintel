Spring Fishing Guide: Canada

Ice-out tactics, early-season hotspots, and gear recommendations for spring fishing across Canada.

Spring is the most exciting time for Canadian anglers. As ice melts and water temperatures rise, fish become aggressively active. This guide covers the best strategies for the spring transition period across Canada's premier fishing destinations.

Ice-Out Strategy

The first open water of the season concentrates fish near shore in warming shallows. Target south-facing bays first — they warm 3-5 degrees faster than north-facing shorelines. Focus on depths of 3-8 feet where baitfish congregate near emerging vegetation. Early morning and late afternoon remain the most productive windows, but overcast days can extend the bite throughout the day.

Top Spring Species & Tactics

Walleye: Move to river mouths and tributaries during spring spawning runs (late April - May). Use jig-and-minnow combinations in 6-12 feet of water. Drag slowly along rocky bottoms near current breaks. Lake Trout: Post-ice-out lakers cruise the shallows (10-20 feet) before retreating to deeper water by mid-June. Troll spoons or cast swimbaits along rocky points. Smallmouth Bass: Target warming flats (55-65°F water temp) with drop-shot rigs or tube jigs. Smallmouth move shallow progressively as water warms. Northern Pike: Spring pike are aggressive in marshes and shallow bays. Cast large spinnerbaits or swim jigs through emerging weed beds.

Essential Spring Gear

Water temperatures are unpredictable in spring. Pack layers including a waterproof shell — sudden rain and wind are common. Neoprene gloves are essential for early mornings. Bring multiple rod setups: a medium-light spinning rod for walleye/bass jigging, and a medium-heavy baitcaster for pike and lake trout trolling. Spring-specific lure colours: chartreuse, white, and natural baitfish patterns tend to outperform in murky spring water.
